Welcome to the Staff Team

Congratulations on becoming a Trial Moderator. During your 7-day trial, you will learn how the server is moderated, help members, and show that you can handle the role responsibly. This handbook covers the basics. Please read it carefully because you will complete a short quiz on Day 5.

1. General Conduct

  • Be respectful to members and other staff, even when conversations become difficult.
  • Stay calm and avoid arguing with members.
  • Treat everyone fairly. Do not give friends special treatment.
  • Use your staff role responsibly and never use permissions for personal reasons.

2. Activity and Communication

  • Be reasonably active and keep an eye on the server when you are available.
  • Join conversations when you can help, but do not interrupt or create arguments.
  • If you expect to be away for an extended period, let Management or a Supervisor know.
  • Ask questions if you are unsure about a rule or what action to take.

3. Moderation Basics

  • Know the server rules before taking moderation action.
  • Check the context and gather the relevant information before acting.
  • Only issue warnings or other punishments when there is a valid reason.
  • If a situation is serious or unclear, ask a Supervisor or higher-ranking staff member for help.
  • Never threaten members or use moderation tools to settle personal disagreements.

4. Handling Tickets

  • Read the member's request before replying.
  • Keep your responses clear, polite, and helpful.
  • Do not enter a ticket just to say “Hi” or “Hey” and then leave without helping.
  • One staff member should normally handle a ticket. Bring in another staff member only when assistance is needed.
  • If you cannot resolve the issue, explain that you are escalating it and contact a Supervisor.

5. Teamwork and Confidentiality

  • Follow reasonable instructions from Supervisors and Management.
  • Keep staff discussions, reports, and private member information within the appropriate staff channels.
  • Do not share screenshots or details from private staff discussions without permission.
  • Report concerns about staff conduct through the proper staff channel rather than arguing publicly.

6. Things You Must Never Do

  • Abuse your permissions or punish someone for personal reasons.
  • Show favoritism or ignore rule-breaking because someone is your friend.
  • Share confidential staff information.
  • Make promises or decisions that are outside your authority.
  • Pretend to know an answer when you are unsure—ask a Supervisor instead.

7. Your 7-Day Trial Schedule

Day 1Read this handbook and become familiar with the server rules.
Day 2Learn your responsibilities and the expectations for Trial Moderators.
Day 3Stay active and practise helping members in the server.
Day 4Learn the basic moderation commands and ticket process.
Day 5Complete the mandatory quiz based on this handbook.
Day 6Demonstrate good judgement, communication, and teamwork.
Day 7Take part in the final performance review with Management.

8. End of Trial

At the end of the 7 days, Management will review your activity, conduct, teamwork, and understanding of the role. You may pass and become a Moderator, have your trial extended if more time is needed, or not pass the trial. The outcome will be communicated to you by Management.

Final Note

Being a moderator is about more than having permissions. It means being fair, helpful, responsible, and willing to learn. You are not expected to know everything straight away. If you are unsure, ask for guidance and learn from the feedback you receive.

9. Understanding Moderation Actions

Moderation actions help keep the server safe and respectful. Always check the situation and follow the server's punishment guidelines before taking action. Trial Moderators should only use actions they have been authorized to use.

Warning (Warn)

A formal reminder that a member has broken a rule. Explain which rule was broken and keep the message polite. Record the warning if the server requires it.

Timeout

Temporarily prevents a member from sending messages or participating in conversations. Use it when a member needs a pause from the chat or has continued breaking rules. Choose a duration that follows the server's guidelines.

Kick

Removes a member from the server, but they may be able to rejoin if they have an invite. This is a more serious action and should only be used when authorized and justified.

Ban

Blocks a member from the server. This is a serious action and should generally be handled by experienced staff or Management unless Trial Moderators have explicitly been given permission for a specific situation.

Strike / Staff Warning

A staff-side consequence for not following staff expectations. It is different from a warning given to a server member and should be handled through the staff process.

10. Before You Punish a Member

  • Read the full conversation and check the context.
  • Make sure a server rule has actually been broken.
  • Check whether the member has already received warnings for the same issue.
  • Use the least severe action that reasonably addresses the situation, following server policy.
  • Give a clear, neutral reason for the action.
  • Keep a record in the appropriate moderation log if required.
  • Ask a Supervisor or Management if you are unsure. Do not guess or act outside your permissions.

11. When to Escalate

Contact a Supervisor or Management for serious threats, harassment, repeated rule-breaking, ban decisions, appeals, or any situation you are not confident handling. Do not promise a particular outcome to a member. Pass on the relevant details and let the appropriate staff member decide what happens next.

Remember

Moderation is about keeping the community safe—not about using the strongest punishment.
